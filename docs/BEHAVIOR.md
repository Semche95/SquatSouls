# Behavior

This document describes how Squat Souls behaves: draws, intensity, limits of an event and of the session, jokers, sessions, history, configuration, language, Steam detection, notification area, sharing and onboarding.

Squat Souls is meant to be kind and encouraging, and to frame without imposing: it gives a structure (draws, limits, jokers, rest), but each user stays free to adjust it and responsible for their training. Every rule below follows from that. This framing is about the people who train, not about the configuration: a technical constraint is not a structure given to someone, and is kept only where the app needs it to work.

It is the reference: any change in the app's behavior must be reflected here, in the same change.

Button and setting names are quoted as they appear in the English interface.

## Confirming actions

- An action is confirmed only once: the button that confirms it (**Create**, confirmation of a removal, **End**, etc.) applies and saves it right away. No action already confirmed should wait for a second confirmation, such as the configuration's **Save** button.
- **Save** is only for changes made directly in the fields, which have no confirmation button of their own.
- Confirmation buttons are never hidden, only disabled when there is nothing to confirm: **Save** and **Discard the changes** stay shown, disabled while there is no pending change.
- Creating or importing a game, importing exercises, and the confirmation of a game removal, of the session's end or cancellation and of quitting open in a dialog over the window. Its button that confirms applies the action and closes it; **Cancel** (or the button that keeps), the × when there is one, Escape or a click outside closes it without changing anything. While it is open, the other shortcuts do nothing. Stopping a set is confirmed on its card instead, so as not to weigh on the training.
- In Program, events and exercises are shown as tiles (a pen tells they can be edited), after a dashed tile that adds one, placed first. A click on a tile opens its edit dialog: the name in a field under the kind of item, one row per setting, then **Delete the event** or **Delete the exercise** and **Close**. A new event or exercise opens its dialog with its name selected. The changes made in the dialog, removal included, wait for **Save** like the other fields; **Close**, Escape or a click outside only closes it.

## Display

- Any section opened or block shown by an action (a nested setting, a confirmation, a message after an action) that ends up partly hidden in the window or under a bar at the bottom of the window (the configuration's save bar, the session buttons) scrolls just enough to show it entirely, or as much of it as fits, starting from its top.
- What appears without an action from the user (update notice, Steam offer, new sets in the queue) never scrolls the window, so the page never moves under them.

## Draw

- Each game offers a list of events. Triggering an event draws `draws` distinct exercises among the enabled exercises (all of them, once each, when fewer are enabled), each with a whole number picked at random between its `min` and its `max` (inclusive).
- Exercises come out in rounds: within a session, each exercise comes out once before any of them can come back. When a round runs out, a new one starts; it does not immediately pick again an exercise of the previous set, nor an exercise already drawn in the same draw (unless it is the only one left to draw, for the first case). A saved configuration restarts the round.
- The range belongs to the exercise: it is the same whatever the event.
- An exercise switched off in the configuration (switch in the corner of its tile, or **Included** in its dialog; a hint under the title of the Exercises tab says so, and the title counts the included exercises out of all of them) stays defined but is no longer drawn, until it is switched on again. The toggle is saved with **Save**, is not carried by exercise sharing, and an imported exercise arrives enabled (or keeps its state if it already existed).
- Every exercise can be switched off or removed, the last one included, as every game can (see Session for what can then be played).
- A draw becomes a set "to do". Sets to do are handled from the oldest to the most recent.
- Selecting an event that gives a set, by its button or its key, briefly lights up the new set in the list and makes the event's counter grow for a moment. Nothing lights up when a limit is reached (see Per-session limit and Session limit), and nothing moves when the session is resumed, reloaded or changed in any other way, nor on coming back to Play. Both animations are off when the system asks for reduced motion.

## Intensity

- The **Intensity** of the session is chosen in Play, next to the game: **Easy**, **Normal** or **Hard**. It scales the number of each exercise drawn, without touching the configuration: half for **Easy**, one and a half for **Hard** (with a range of 10 to 20, **Easy** gives 5 to 10 and **Hard** 15 to 30).
- **Easy** always rounds down, without falling to 0, and **Normal** and **Hard** always round up, **Halve** included: 15 gives 7 in **Easy** and 23 in **Hard**, and a 9 halved gives 4 in **Easy** and 5 otherwise.
- It can change during the session and, as a changed minimum or maximum does, applies right away to the exercises to do: their number is the one drawn, halved first if it was, scaled by the new intensity (18 drawn gives 27 in **Hard** and 9 in **Easy**). Exercises already done never change.
- Hovering a level shows its effect on the number of exercises.
- It appears neither in the summary nor in the history, which show the numbers actually done.
- Each launch starts at **Normal**, unless **Remember the intensity between launches** is on in Settings (off by default, part of the configuration and saved with **Save**): the chosen intensity is then kept in the database, outside the configuration, from one launch to the next. A session resumed at launch keeps its own intensity either way; once it ends, however it ends, the intensity is the one the launch gives (**Normal**, or the remembered one), unless an intensity was chosen during the session.

## Per-session limit

- Each trigger of an event is counted for the session, per game and per event.
- Beyond `maxPerSession` triggers, the event no longer gives a set. The trigger counter then stays stuck at the limit and fades, and a tooltip on the tile says the event asks for nothing more until the next session; the tile stays selectable. Selecting it, by its button or its key, shows "Limit reached" over the tile for a moment and shakes its counter, without moving the rest of the screen; screen readers announce it, and the counter does not shake when the system asks for reduced motion. This cue is not replayed when the session is resumed or reloaded, nor on coming back to Play, and ends with the session.

## Session limit

- Next to the intensity, Play offers a **Limit** for the session: **None** (by default), **Exercises**, **Play time** or **Training time**, in a list whose width does not change with the choice. Once a kind other than **None** is chosen, its value shows next to it, a number of exercises or of minutes from 1 to 9999, with its - and + buttons; each kind starts from its own value when chosen (20 exercises, 60 min of play, 30 min of training). **None** removes the limit and hides the value. A badly filled value is not applied: the last one filled correctly stays.
- **Exercises** counts the exercises asked, from the trigger of their set, as the per-session limit of an event counts its triggers. **Play time** counts the time of the session, without the time the app was closed. **Training time** counts the estimated time of the exercises asked, from the trigger of their set: for a timed exercise, its duration with the countdown before the timer, twice when it is done on each side; otherwise its repetitions at the **Duration of a repetition**; then its recovery (its own, otherwise the global one).
- **Duration of a repetition**, with the global recovery in the **Exercise settings** dialog, opened by the button at the top left of the Exercises sub-tab of Program, which shows both values (**Recovery 60 s · Repetition 3 s**), applies to every exercise at once and cannot be set for a single exercise: 3 s in the default configuration, from 1 to 3600 s, saved with **Save** and not carried by exercise sharing.
- A set that would go beyond a limit in exercises or in training time keeps only its first exercises that fit; for a limit in training time, the limit is then reached, even with some time left. Once the limit is reached, or the play time over, the events no longer give a set and their trigger is not counted: selecting one shows "Limit reached" over its tile as the per-session limit of an event does, and the tiles' tooltip says the session limit is reached and that raising it brings sets back. The sets to do stay to do, and the session ends as usual.
- A dismissed set no longer counts, and frees the room it took when triggered, even once a saved configuration changed it; a stopped set, or one whose exercises were skipped with jokers, stays counted. A set given back from the reserve is reduced like a new one, the exercises left out going back to the round. A saved configuration or a changed intensity never recounts what was asked, and never adds exercises to a set the limit reduced.
- A session saved before the update that brought the limit resumes without a limit, and what it already asked is counted again at launch: its sets to do as drawn, and for its finished sets as many exercises as their event draws, those done at their number and the others (skipped or stopped) at the middle of their range on average.
- The limit can change at any time, during the session too: a higher limit, or none, lets the events give sets again; a lower one removes nothing already asked.
- During a session with a limit, the progress shows next to the jokers ("7 / 20 exercises", "18 / 45 min") with a gauge, faded once the limit is reached; its tooltip says what it counts.
- Each launch starts without a limit, unless **Remember the session limit between launches** is on in Settings (off by default, part of the configuration and saved with **Save**): the chosen limit is then kept in the database, outside the configuration, from one launch to the next. A session resumed at launch keeps its own limit either way; once it ends, however it ends, the limit is the one the launch gives (none, or the remembered one), unless a limit was chosen during the session.

## Handling a set to do

- Sets are done in the order they were drawn: only the oldest offers **Done** and the jokers (the backend refuses them on the others); the next ones, queued, can only be dismissed.
- The queue shows each set waiting as a compact row: its event, the jokers it earns and **Cancel**, then its exercises below, one per line. Past 3 sets waiting, the next ones are folded behind a "+N sets waiting" button, which shows them in the same order and then reads **Collapse**. A set added to the folded part lights up this button instead of the set. Folding is display only and never saved: the queue is folded again on coming back to Play, and once 3 sets or fewer are left waiting.
- **Done** is marked exercise by exercise, in the order of the set: only its first remaining exercise offers it (the backend refuses the others), while the jokers stay offered on every remaining exercise. The exercise is added to the totals and written to the history, and stays displayed struck through until the end of the set. The set moves to the sets done when its last exercise is done or skipped, and the event's jokers are earned at that moment.
- **Cancel** a set not started yet (misclick): the set disappears and the trigger is uncounted, as if it never happened. The dismissed set is reserved: the next trigger of the same event gives back the same exercises with the same numbers, as drawn, so that dismissing cannot be used to change the draw. The reserve disappears with the session (end or cancellation); a saved configuration adjusts it like the sets to do.
- **Stop** a started set, after confirmation (**Stop the set**): its exercises already done stay in the totals and the history, the rest of the set disappears. The trigger stays counted, including for the per-session limit, the set earns no jokers and nothing is reserved. Stopping is free, even after a single exercise: each user is responsible for their training, and stopping at the slightest pain must cost nothing.
- **Jokers**: halve or skip an exercise, or skip the whole set (see Jokers). The trigger stays counted, including for the per-session limit.

## Recovery between exercises

- Each exercise done (**Done**) starts a recovery: its own recovery, or otherwise the global recovery (60 s in the default configuration). A 0 s recovery starts none.
- During the recovery, no exercise can be marked done (tooltip "Recovery in progress" on **Done**), neither in the same set nor in another one, including a set drawn during the recovery. Jokers, **Cancel** and **Stop** remain possible and do not start a recovery.
- When the recovery reaches its end, a bell rings twice, like the start of a boxing round. Ending it early or stopping it plays nothing.
- The recovery shows as a countdown on the top border of the current set (or of the empty block), its ring emptying as time passes. Clicking it (tooltip **Skip**) or pressing Escape ends the recovery right away. It also stops when the session ends or is cancelled, but not when the set whose exercise started it stops: that exercise stays done.
- A configuration saved during the recovery (global recovery, the exercise's own recovery, or the exercise removed) gives the recovery running the duration that now applies to the exercise that started it, as if it had started with it: the countdown shows that duration minus the time already elapsed, longer or shorter. Already past that duration, the recovery ends right away, with the bell, or without a sound if that duration is 0 s. If the same save ends the session, the recovery stops with it, without a sound.

## Timer

- In Program, each exercise has a **Timer** choice: **Not timed** (default), **Seconds** or **Minutes**. The number of a timed exercise is a duration in that unit; its unit stays free text, only shown. The default configuration times the planks, the arm circles and the hip stretch. On the update that brought the timer, existing exercises whose unit reads as a duration (`s`, `sec`, `seconds`, `secondes`, `min`, `minutes`...) became timed, once: afterwards only the choice counts.
- A timed exercise also offers **Repeat for the other side** (off by default; on for the hip stretch in the default configuration). Its hint says that the duration is for one side and that the timer runs again for the other side. Removing the timer turns it off. The stopwatch on the exercise's tile shows it is timed, and its tooltip recalls the unit and the sides ("Timed in seconds, on each side").
- **Timer** (or T) is offered next to **Done** on the first remaining exercise of the current set, when it is timed. It runs the exercise's current number, halved included. It cannot start during the recovery.
- While the timer runs or is held, **Done** and **Timer** are disabled (tooltip "Timer running"); the jokers, **Cancel** and **Stop** remain possible.
- The timer shows in the recovery's countdown, in ember: first the countdown before the timer ("Ready"), then the exercise's duration. Clicking it or Space holds or resumes it (Space never marks an exercise done); Escape stops it, and it starts over from the beginning at the next start. It also stops as soon as its exercise is no longer the one to do as it was: halved or skipped, its set skipped, dismissed or stopped, the session cancelled.
- On each side, the timer runs the countdown, the first side, then the countdown again to switch sides ("Other side"), then the second side, without any recovery in between: it is the same exercise. While a side runs, the countdown shows it ("Side 1/2", "Side 2/2"). Escape stops the side in progress, and the next start resumes at its beginning: once the first side is over, it stays done and the timer resumes at the second side. A stop because the exercise is no longer the one to do as it was forgets the first side: halved, the exercise starts over from the first side at half duration.
- A short low note plays when the timer starts, after the countdown; on each side, two short notes on the same pitch mark the end of the first side. At the end, two rising notes play and the exercise is marked done, which starts the recovery. The **Timer** group of Settings turns off the marking (**Mark the exercise done when the timer ends**, on by default: **Done** is then available again), and sets the **Countdown before the timer** with its field or its - and + buttons (3 s by default, none at 0). These settings are saved with **Save**.

## Sound

- **Play sounds**, in the **General** group of Settings (on by default, with its uses recalled underneath), turned off, silences every sound: those of the timer and the one at the end of the recovery. On the update that brought it, it took the value of the former timer sound setting. It is saved with **Save**.

## Jokers

- Only events with `jokers` earn some, once all the exercises of their set are done: spending a joker on a set (half or skip) makes it lose those it was meant to earn.
- **Halve** (1 joker): divides an exercise's number by two, rounded as the intensity does (down in **Easy**, up otherwise). Only once per exercise, and not for a number of 1.
- **Skip** an exercise (2 jokers, only 1 if it was already halved or cannot be, its number being 1): it is removed from the set, the rest remains to do. Skipping the last exercise removes the set.
- **Skip the set** (set with several remaining exercises): costs the sum of the skips of its remaining exercises; those already done stay done.
- An exercise done can no longer be halved or skipped.
- An action is only possible with enough jokers. What is removed is not done (neither totals nor history). The History tab only shows the jokers earned and used: the sets done list what was done, at its real number, without mentioning any joker.
- Unspent jokers are lost at the end of the session.

## Session

- A session does not start on its own, so that its duration only counts the time played: it starts with **Start the session**, shown in place of **End the session** until then, or with its first triggered event. **Start the session** stays disabled until a game is chosen (tooltip "Choose a game", or "No game with events" when none can be chosen), since nothing could be played. Without any included exercise, no session can start: **Start the session** and the events are disabled (tooltip "No exercise included", the backend refuses them too, keyboard shortcuts included), and Play says that exercises are created and included in Program, a link to its Exercises sub-tab. After each end or cancellation, the next session waits to be started, as it does when the app opens without a session in progress.
- As soon as a session starts, with **Start the session** or with its first triggered event, the game choice of Play is disabled (tooltip "The game can't be changed during a session"): the session's game can no longer change before the next session. The last chosen game is remembered from one launch to the next. On the very first launch, or if that game no longer exists or has no event left, no game is selected: it must be chosen before starting the session or triggering an event. A game without event is saved, but the game choice shows it greyed out with "(no event)" and does not let it be chosen, nor the Steam detection select it. Every game can be removed, the last one included: without any game, the game choice of Play is disabled and Play says that games are created and imported in Program, a link to its Games sub-tab, and Program only offers **New game** and **Import**.
- The session in progress is saved at each change (sets triggered, exercises done, jokers, dismissed or stopped sets, intensity, limit, saved configuration), and the time every minute. When the app closes during a session, after quitting as after a crash, the session resumes at the next launch as it was: its game, its intensity, its limit, its sets to do and done, its counters, its jokers, its reserve and its round. A running timer starts over from the beginning, as after Escape, and a running recovery counts as over, without the bell; a started set stays started. If the saved session cannot be read back, the app opens without it: it ends when the app was last known to be open, its exercises done staying in the history, and Play says "The session ended: it could not be resumed. Its exercises done stay in the history." until the next session starts.
- **End the session** is only possible with at least one set done and no set to do (the backend refuses it otherwise). The end time is saved in the history.
- **Cancel the session** resets everything (counters, jokers, sets to do and done) and removes the sets of this session from the history. The button stays disabled until the session has started. It asks for confirmation once the session has a set, done or to do; without any (for example after dismissing the only set), nothing is lost and it cancels right away.
- The session buttons (**Start the session** or **End the session**, **Cancel the session**) sit under the sets to do; once the list runs past the bottom of the window, they stay at the bottom of the window, always visible, as the Program and Settings save bar does. A line above them sets them apart from the sets only while they sit at the bottom of the window.
- A saved configuration applies immediately, without restarting. It touches neither the sets done nor the counters (renaming a game or an event keeps its counters). The sets to do stay as drawn, with up-to-date names, except:
  - a removed event removes its sets to do;
  - a changed number of drawn exercises (`draws`) adjusts its sets: the missing exercises are drawn, or exercises not done yet are removed; a set with nothing left to do is finished and earns its jokers;
  - a removed or disabled exercise is replaced by another enabled exercise drawn at random, absent from the set; without any, it leaves the set, and a set with nothing left to do is finished and earns its jokers;
  - a changed minimum or maximum brings the number of an exercise to do back within the new bounds (scaled by the intensity), unless it was halved;
  - changed jokers apply to the event's sets, except those on which jokers were already spent, which no longer earn any.

  Exercises already done never change. If it removes the game of the current session, or leaves no exercise included, the session ends there, as if each set to do were dismissed: a set not started is no longer counted, a started set stops, its exercises done staying in the history with the session's summary and end time. Without any exercise done, nothing is saved, as with **Cancel the session**. Either way, Play says why the session ended ("The session ended: its game was deleted." or "The session ended: no exercise is included anymore.") until the next session starts. When it removes the game, the game must then be chosen again; when it only leaves no exercise included, the game stays selected. If it leaves the game of the current session without event, the session keeps it, still shown in Play, and can still end with its sets done.

## History and summary

- Each exercise done is written to the history (one entry per exercise), with the time, the session, the game, the event, the exercise and the amount. It is kept from one launch to the next.
- The summary groups sets by session, under the local day the session started (a session past midnight stays whole on that day), then by game, with totals per exercise (cumulated amount and number of sets). Each session shows its start time.
- The History tab shows the last 7 days, rest days included. **See all** opens the full history over the whole window: only the days with sets, most recent first, loaded 60 days at a time, the older ones as its end comes into view, so it opens right away however long the history is.
- A session's duration only counts the time the app was open: from its start, then from each reopening, until the app closed (to the minute after a crash), and until its end time once ended, or else its last set. Sessions saved before this was recorded go from their start to their end time if ended, otherwise to their last set.
- The summary stays positive: it shows what was done, never what was skipped. The history counts the days of activity since the beginning (days on which a session with sets done started), a number that only goes up: no streak of consecutive days that could be lost, and a day without a set reads "rest".
- Once a session ends with exercises done, whether with **End the session** or by saving Program or deleting the session's game (see above), the Play tab shows an encouragement picked at random, translated, above the session buttons, until the next session starts. A cancelled session shows none.
- **Copy the summary** copies to the clipboard a text to paste in a messaging app, for the current session (once a set is done) and for each session of the history. One line per item: the game and the duration, the events triggered with their number (in the order of the game's events), the total per exercise, then the jokers earned and used. The current session lasts until the copy, without the time the app was closed. The events and jokers of each session are saved in the history as the session goes, as if it stopped there: the event of a set to do not started yet, and the jokers spent on it, are not counted until an exercise of it is done, so that the summary keeps only what was done (the event's limit, though, counts it from its trigger); sessions saved before this was recorded leave out those two lines.
- **Copy the day** copies in the same way a whole day of the history, once it has at least two sessions: the day, each session's summary preceded by its start time, then the day's total per exercise.

## Configuration

- **Program** (games, events, exercises) and **Settings** are separate: each tab's **Save** and **Discard the changes** only apply to its own changes. As long as the tab has some, **Unsaved changes** shows next to them.
- The configuration is validated before being saved. It is refused if: an id or a name is empty, an id is duplicated, two games have the same name (ignoring case and surrounding spaces), `draws` is 0, a number is out of its bounds (see below), a name (game, event, exercise) exceeds 300 characters or a unit 50, a name or unit contains a control or invisible character (line break, tab, zero width character, text direction override; the zero width joiner of emoji sequences is allowed), or there are more than 100 exercises or more than 100 events in a game. The name and unit fields do not accept more characters than allowed.
- Each number has bounds, far above any training, that keep it within its frame on screen: the minimum and maximum of an exercise go from 1 to 9999, the minimum never above the maximum; the limit per session from 1 to 9999; the jokers earned from 0 to 9999; the requested exercises from 1 to 100; the recovery times and the countdown before the timer from 0 to 3600 s; the duration of a repetition from 1 to 3600 s.
- A configuration saved before these limits is brought within them at load rather than refused: control and invisible characters are removed, names and units are cut at the limit, numbers out of their bounds are brought to the nearest one (a minimum or a duration of a repetition at 0 becomes 1, a maximum below the minimum rises to it), exercises and events beyond 100 are dropped, and a name left empty takes its id. It is saved this way at the next **Save**.
- Each number field (recovery times, duration of a repetition, countdown before the timer, minimum, maximum, requested exercises, limit, jokers) must be a whole number within its bounds. This is checked while typing: a badly filled field (text, decimal number, number out of its bounds) gets a red border and the problem shows right away in a tooltip while typing; once the field is left, the tooltip closes and reopens on hover. Other errors and confirmations only appear on save; an error stays until the changes are edited or discarded. While a field is badly filled, **Save** is disabled and its tooltip names the first faulty field (for example "Squats › minimum: at most 15."). A cleared required field is 0, a cleared optional field (own recovery, limit) is removed.
- Each number field also has - and + buttons. - stops at the lowest value of its bounds and + at the highest, the minimum of an exercise at its maximum and the maximum at its minimum, their tooltip saying why they are disabled; on an optional field, - removes the value below its lowest (the limit under 1, the own recovery under 0). + on an empty optional field starts from the value it stands for (the global recovery) or from 1 (the limit).
- An event or exercise name left empty gets a red border and a message in its dialog, its tile shows "(unnamed)", and **Save** stays disabled, its tooltip naming the field (for example "Elden Ring › event name: it cannot be empty.").
- Games are always listed by name, ignoring case and accents, in the Play tab as in the Program tab. Without a chosen game, the configuration opens on the first one in the list.
- In Program, the game is chosen in a select, like in Play. **Rename** turns it into a text field with the caret at the end of the name: **Done** (or Enter) applies the new name, which waits for **Save** like the other changes, and **Cancel** (or Esc) keeps the old one. **Done** is disabled while the name is empty or already taken by another game.
- A database just created (first launch) receives the default configuration, described in `src-tauri/src/app/seed.en.json` and `seed.fr.json` (same ids, numbers and AppIds, only the names and units differ), in the effective language at that launch (see Language). An existing database never receives it, even without any game. If the saved configuration is unreadable or invalid, the default configuration in the effective language is used and the error is shown; a session resumed at that launch adjusts to it as to a saved configuration.

## Language

- The **Language** setting, at the top of the **General** group of Settings, offers three choices side by side: **System language** (default), **English** and **Français**. The effective language is always English or French.
- With the system language, the effective language is French if the system locale starts with `fr` (`fr`, `fr-FR`, `fr_CA.UTF-8`...), English otherwise. It is resolved by the backend at each launch, never frozen in the database. A missing or unknown saved value counts as the system language.
- The setting is saved in the database outside the configuration: a change applies right away (interface, notification area menu, notifications) and is saved immediately, without going through **Save** and without touching the pending changes. If saving fails, the error is shown and the list goes back to the saved value.
- Dates, times and the alphabetical order of games follow the effective language. Data (names of games, events and exercises, units) is never translated: the default configuration is only chosen at the first launch, and a language change never modifies an existing database.
- The backend holds no translated text: it only sends error codes with their values, and the interface translates them (a generic message for an unknown code). Technical errors (database, files) only show a generic message, their detail goes to the logs. The notification area menu and the notifications use texts sent by the interface: until it has sent them, the backend creates neither the icon nor a notification.

## Steam game detection

- Optional, disabled by default: the **Detect the running Steam game** toggle in Settings is part of the configuration and is saved with **Save**.
- When enabled, the app reads the processes every 10 seconds and looks for the AppId of a game launched by Steam (`SteamLaunch AppId=…` in its command line, or `SteamAppId` in its environment). Without a result and if a Steam process is running, it takes the AppId Steam records for the running game (`RunningAppID` in the registry on Windows, in `registry.vdf` on macOS and Linux), for games whose environment is unreadable. Only Steam games are detected, and no game list is bundled.
- A configured game is recognized by its Steam AppId. Several games can carry the same one: a game in the configuration is a challenge setup, and the same Steam game can have several. A game without AppId is recognized by its name, which it repeats or extends without an adjoining letter ("Megabonk 2" for "Megabonk"), ignoring case and surrounding spaces: the name of the games carrying the detected AppId, or otherwise the one given by the Steam Store. The games of the default configuration have their AppId, except Bloodborne, absent from Steam.
- Configured game: the Play tab offers one button per recognized game to select it, unless one of them already is or the session's game is locked. Recognized by its name, it then receives the detected AppId, saved right away.
- Unknown game: its name is asked from the Steam Store API in the interface language (without the ™ and ® marks, the control and invisible characters, and cut at 300 characters like any name; a name already obtained is kept, even after a language change), then the Play tab offers to **Add** or **Ignore** it. **Add** opens game creation, prefilled with that name and the AppId, which is saved with **Create**. **Ignore** adds it right away to the ignored games, which are never offered again.
- Settings lists the ignored games, folded by default behind their number and scrolling past 10: **Remove** removes it from the list right away, and it will be offered again.
- To never get blocked by Steam, the Steam Store is only called for an unknown, non-ignored game: a name obtained is never asked again; a game it did not name, or a request left unanswered, is asked again an hour later at the earliest; at most one call per minute, with a 10 s maximum wait. If it refuses for too many requests (status 429 or 403), it is not called for 24 h: unknown games are no longer offered, configured games are still detected. These delays and the names obtained are kept in the database, from one launch to the next.
- **Notify when a game is launched**, visible under the detection toggle when it is on, and on by default, sends a system notification only once per launch: for a configured game ("Select it in Play to start the session.") or an unknown one ("add it from Play") once its name is known, never for an ignored game nor when one of the games recognized is already selected: selecting another one then does not notify it until it is relaunched. A game relaunched after no game has been running is notified again. It is saved with **Save**.
- Under the toggle, Settings shows the status of the saved detection: the detected game (even if already selected or ignored), no game, or a Steam Store rate-limiting requests. If the processes are unreadable, nothing is detected; the next scan tries again.

## Notification area

- Optional, disabled by default: the **Show the icon in the notification area** toggle in Settings is part of the configuration and is saved with **Save**, which adds or removes the icon right away.
- When on, it unfolds two toggles, off by default: **Closing the window hides it instead of quitting** (the app and Steam detection keep running) and **Start hidden** (the app starts without a window). Without the icon, they have no effect: closing the window quits the app, which always starts with its window shown.
- A click on the icon shows the window; its menu offers **Show** and **Quit** (on Linux, only the menu is available).
- **Ctrl+Q** (**Cmd+Q** on macOS) quits the app completely, from any tab, even when closing the window only hides it.
- During a session (started and not ended), quitting asks for confirmation in a dialog (see Confirming actions): **Ctrl+Q**, **Quit** in the icon menu (which shows the window) and closing the window when it quits the app. Nothing is lost: the dialog says the session will resume where it left off at the next launch. **Quit** or a second **Ctrl+Q** quits, **Stay** closes the dialog.

## Sharing

- A game is shared on its own: its name, its Steam AppId if it has one, and its events (drawn number, limit, jokers), without the exercises, which are personal. A game without event cannot be shared. Exercises are shared separately, all of them with **Share** in the Exercises tab or one with **Share** in its dialog (an exercise not saved yet cannot be): name, unit, minimum, maximum, own recovery, timer and whether it is done on each side. The global recovery, the duration of a repetition and the timer settings are not shared.
- **Share** and **Import** (games and exercises alike) are disabled while Program has pending changes (tooltip "Save or discard the changes first"): a share would leave them out, and an import, saved right away, would have to merge with them. The pending changes of Settings do not block them.
- **Share** copies to the clipboard a compressed text to pass on (unreadable as is, it withstands the line breaks added by messaging apps), taken from the saved program. It starts with a short identifier that tells the content: `squatsouls/game/elden-ring:`, `squatsouls/exercises:` or `squatsouls/exercise/squats:`. A single exercise is imported like a list of exercises. This identifier is only informative: the import ignores it and reads the format version and the content type in the compressed text. The import reads each text according to its version and refuses, asking to update the app, a version it does not know. In a text shared before exercises could be timed, an exercise whose unit reads as a duration is timed, as on the update that brought the timer. In a text shared before the numbers were bounded, the numbers out of the bounds are brought within them, as the configurations were on the update that brought the bounds: a minimum of 0 becomes 1, a maximum, a limit or jokers over 9999 become 9999, an own recovery over an hour becomes an hour.
- **Import** opens a dialog asking to paste this text and is only possible if it is valid. A pasted text is never trusted: it must follow the configuration limits (names, units, number bounds, number of exercises and events, characters), and a text or decompressed content over 256 KiB is refused without being fully decompressed. Two events of the game, or two exercises, with the same name (ignoring case and surrounding spaces) do not block the import: only the first is kept. A game pasted into the exercise import, or the reverse, is refused with a message that says where to import it. The import is saved right away, without going through **Save**; the pending changes of Settings stay pending.
- Once the text is valid, the dialog lists what the import changes, each item with a switch, grouped under **New**, **Changed** and, for a replaced game, **Not in the import**; identical items are not listed. A changed item shows only its fields that change, old value then new one, and is accepted or refused as a whole. New and changed items start checked; a group of several items has an **All** switch, on when all its items are. **Import** needs at least one change applied: when nothing differs, the dialog says there is nothing to import.
- An imported game either replaces a game chosen in **Game to replace** (**Replace a game**) or is added under a name to give (**Import as a new game**), the shared name not always being the one used in the app. When the name is taken (ignoring case and surrounding spaces), the dialog says so and replacing the game with that name is the default, the new game's name being prefilled with the first free one among "Elden Ring 2", "Elden Ring 3"…; otherwise importing as a new game under the shared name is the default, replacing then offering the first game by name. Without any game, **Replace a game** is disabled. A new game lists all its events as new. Replacing keeps the game's name, its place, and its Steam AppId when the import brings none. Its events follow the import's order: a checked event is added or replaces the one with the same name, which keeps its name; an unchecked one leaves the game's event as it is. The game's events missing from the import are listed unchecked, and removed unless checked again, in which case they stay at the end. An event that requests more than 100 exercises is capped at 100, and the confirmation message reports it when a checked event was capped.
- A checked exercise updates the unit, minimum, maximum, own recovery, timer and each side of the one with the same name (without an own recovery in the import, the exercise goes back to the global recovery; without a timer, it is no longer timed; without each side, it is done once), or is added; no exercise is removed.

## Update

- At launch, the installed app looks for a newer version among the published releases. Offline or when the check fails, nothing is shown, and a banner already shown goes away: only a warning is logged. The development app never looks for one.
- The **Updates** group, last in Settings, holds two settings, also shown in the development app, where they change nothing, and saved with **Save**:
  - **Check for updates** (on by default), turned off, the app no longer looks for one: the check, the download and the downloaded update are dropped, and nothing is installed on quitting. Turned back on, the app looks for one right away.
  - **Update without asking**, nested under it and only shown while it is on (on by default): the version found is downloaded in the background right away. Turned off, the banner announces it with an **Update** button that starts the download, and nothing is downloaded until then. Changing it drops what was found and looks for one again right away.
- As soon as a newer version is found, a banner at the top of the window announces it; while it downloads, a bar follows the download. When the download fails, the banner stays: it says so, with a **Try again** button that downloads it again, whether the download was automatic or asked for. Once downloaded, the banner says the version will be installed on quitting, with a **Restart now** button that installs it and restarts the app. During a session, the button is disabled (the backend refuses it too), and its tooltip says it is available once the session is over.
- On Linux, the `.deb` and `.rpm` packages, whose installation would ask for the system password, are only told: the banner announces the version with a **Download** button that opens the page of the latest release, and nothing is downloaded.
- Quitting the app (whatever the way) installs the downloaded update, without restarting it, unless a session is in progress: the session resumes at the next launch, and the update waits for the app to quit without one, as the banner says during the session. An update never interrupts a session.
- The database is kept from one version to the next: the new version applies its missing migrations at launch.

## Launch

- The app runs only once: launching it again, with its window hidden in the notification area for instance, shows the window already open. The development app is apart and can run beside the installed one.
- At each launch, the database is checked, then copied for the next launch before receiving its migrations. While the app runs, the copy is renewed every hour, as long as the database is still intact.
- A damaged database is set aside, only the 3 most recent ones being kept, and replaced by the copy of the previous launch if that copy is intact. The app then opens as usual, with a banner, closed by its cross: "A problem prevented reading the data. The copy saved at the previous launch was restored, so the latest changes may be missing."
- When the database cannot be opened, the app opens a small minimal window instead of quitting: the title, the reason in the system language, and under it the update banner. The database is left as it is. As the update settings are in the database, the app looks for an update and downloads it as when they are on, and installs it on quitting or with **Restart now**. The reasons:
  - the version could not prepare the data (a migration failed, or the data comes from a newer version): nothing was changed and a fix will come with an update; **Report the problem on GitHub** opens a new issue of the repository, and as long as the banner shows no update, **Get the latest version on GitHub** opens the page of the latest release;
  - the file is damaged without an intact copy of the previous launch: it was left as it is; **Report the problem on GitHub**;
  - the app cannot write its data (read-only file or folder, or another program using it): **Try again** launches the app again once fixed;
  - the disk is full: **Try again** as well.
- Every reason also offers **Quit**.

## About

- **About**, last entry of the menu (at the bottom of the sidebar, right above the session status), opens a window without leaving the current tab: a description of the app, its author, its version, a notice that the project is not affiliated with any game publisher, the **Safety advice** of the onboarding, and a **Source code** button, which opens the repository in the default browser. Its cross in the top right corner or Escape closes it.

## Onboarding

- At the first launch, existing databases included, the onboarding covers the whole window, in four steps shown one at a time, with **Back** and **Next**:
  - the concept of the app in one line;
  - **Craft your build**: the user fills in the program, and the exercises already there are only examples to adjust before starting;
  - **Take care of yourself**: a doctor's advice if in doubt, warming up and stretching, form before numbers, splitting long sets, breaks and water, stopping at the slightest pain;
  - **No need to git gud**: playing badly is not punished, doing more than planned brings nothing, taking a break is allowed.
- It can only be left from its last step: **See the program** opens the exercises of the **Program** tab, **Let's go** stays on the current tab. It is then never shown again, and its safety advice stays in **About**.
